# KrishiAtlas — Master Agricultural Land Market & Affordability Research

This register documents agricultural land ownership regulations, purchase restrictions for non-farmers, land tenure laws, circle rates vs. market prices, lease markets, due diligence protocols, and hidden development costs across major Indian states.

## 1. Statutory Purchase Restrictions: Can a Non-Farmer Buy Agricultural Land?

The legal ability to purchase agricultural land in India is strictly governed by State-specific land revenue and agrarian reform statutes under Entry 18 of the State List (Seventh Schedule of the Indian Constitution).

| State | Non-Farmer Purchase Permitted? | Governing Act / Section | Specific Restrictions & Exceptions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Uttar Pradesh** | **YES** (with area ceilings) | UP Revenue Code, 2006 (Section 89) | Any Indian citizen may purchase up to **12.5 acres (5.0586 hectares)** of agricultural land without prior administrative permission. Acquisition exceeding 12.5 acres requires prior permission of the State Government. |
| **Himachal Pradesh** | **NO** (Strictly Restricted) | HP Tenancy and Land Reforms Act, 1972 (Section 118) | Non-agriculturists of HP cannot purchase agricultural land. Non-state residents can only purchase small residential plots or obtain commercial/industrial leasehold under Section 118 with Cabinet approval. |
| **Uttarakhand** | **RESTRICTED** | UP Zamindari Abolition and Land Reforms Act (as adapted by UK) | Outside municipal areas, an outsider/non-agriculturist may purchase up to **250 sq. metres** without prior permission for residential use. Bulk agricultural purchases require District Magistrate (DM) permission. |
| **Maharashtra** | **CONDITIONAL** | Maharashtra Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act (Section 63) | Historically restricted to agriculturists. Amending Act allows non-agriculturists with income up to ₹7.5 lakh to purchase land for agriculture, or for bonafide industrial/commercial use under Section 63-1A. |
| **Karnataka** | **YES** (Reformed) | Karnataka Land Reforms (Amendment) Act, 2020 | Sections 79A, 79B, and 79C (which previously barred non-agriculturists and capped non-agricultural income) were repealed in 2020. Indian citizens can now purchase agricultural land up to prescribed ceiling limits. |
| **Kerala** | **YES** (Subject to Ceilings) | Kerala Land Reforms Act, 1963 | Non-agriculturists can purchase, subject to statutory family ceilings (typically 15 acres). |
| **Tamil Nadu** | **YES** | Tamil Nadu Land Reforms (Fixation of Ceiling on Land) Act, 1961 | No agriculturist-only restriction; ceiling of 15 standard acres for a family of up to 5 members. |
| **Rajasthan** | **YES** | Rajasthan Tenancy Act, 1955 | Any Indian citizen can purchase non-tribal agricultural land up to ceiling limits. |
| **Punjab & Haryana** | **YES** | Punjab Land Reforms Act, 1972 / Haryana Ceiling on Land Holdings Act, 1972 | No agriculturist-only restriction. Strict ceiling limits (typically 7.05 to 21.8 hectares depending on assured irrigation). |
| **Tribal Areas / Scheduled Areas (Pan-India)** | **ABSOLUTE BAR** | 5th Schedule, CNT Act (Jharkhand), SPT Act, AP Land Transfer Regulation 1/70 | Non-tribals are legally prohibited from purchasing land belonging to Scheduled Tribes (ST) across all Scheduled Areas, under penalty of land restoration and prosecution. |

## 2. Circle Rate (Guideline Value) vs. Actual Market Value

### Understanding Circle Rates
- **Circle Rate (Jantri / DLC / Ready Reckoner / Guideline Value):** The minimum baseline valuation fixed by the District Magistrate / State Revenue Department for calculating Stamp Duty and Registration Fees.
- **The Divergence:** In peri-urban corridors (e.g. Ghaziabad, Gautam Buddha Nagar, Gurugram, Pune), market transaction prices may exceed circle rates by 1.5x to 4x. In remote rural/dryland zones (e.g., parts of Bundelkhand or Vidarbha), market prices can occasionally sit near or below circle rates due to lack of irrigation and distress selling.
- **Tax Trap under Section 56(2)(x) & Section 50C:**
  - If a buyer purchases land at a price *lower* than the Stamp Duty Value (circle rate) by more than 10%, the difference is treated as taxable income under "Income from Other Sources" in the hands of the buyer u/s 56(2)(x).
  - Simultaneously, the seller is deemed to have received the full Stamp Duty Value for capital gains tax computation u/s 50C.

## 3. The Fallacy of "Cheap Land" — Hidden Capital Expenditure

Promotional land listings often market remote agricultural land at ₹2–₹5 lakh per acre in central India or arid belts. A financial model that accounts only for purchase price will fail because developability costs dwarf acquisition costs:

| Factor | Typical Cost / Reality in Low-Cost Land | Economic Impact on Capital |
|---|---|---|
| **Right of Way (Rasta / Approach Road)** | Often landlocked (chak road not carved out). Purchasing access easement from neighbouring farmers costs 2x–5x base rate. | Tractor/harvester cannot enter; crop inputs and harvest must be manually headloaded. |
| **Groundwater / Borewell Drilling** | Arid / hard-rock basalt or granite aquifers often yield dry holes. Drilling 600–1,000 ft + casing = ₹2.0–₹3.5 lakh per borewell with zero guarantee. | Without water, intensive agriculture is impossible; restricted to single-season rainfed pulses/millets. |
| **Three-Phase Agricultural Power** | Rural electricity transformer installation: ₹1.5–₹4.0 lakh; multi-year waitlist for dedicated agri-connections. | Forces reliance on expensive diesel generator sets (₹35–₹45/hour running cost) or solar pump capex. |
| **Fencing & Wild Animal Protection** | Nilgai (blue bull), wild boar, and stray cattle ruin open crops. 7-strand barbed wire + RCC poles = ₹1.5–₹2.5 lakh per acre. | Total crop loss risk without fencing in peri-forest or fallow-heavy corridors. |
| **Soil Remediation & Land Levelling** | Bulldozer levelling, boulder clearing, and gypsum/organic manure amendment for alkaline/saline soils: ₹40,000–₹1,00,000/acre. | Suppressed yields and stunted germination for first 2–3 seasons. |
| **Remote Supervision & Theft** | Absentee farm owners suffer high pilferage of motors, cables, sprinklers, and harvested produce; local caretaker wages: ₹1.2–₹1.8 LPA. | Destroys margins on smallholdings (< 10 acres). |

## 4. Land Lease Models: Cash Rent vs. Sharecropping (Batai)

For a beginner or salaried professional, **leasing in land** or **leasing out existing family land** provides a low-risk alternative to capital purchase:

1. **Fixed Cash Rent (Theka / Pattedari):**
   - The tenant pays an agreed cash sum per acre per year (paid upfront 50% in Kharif, 50% in Rabi).
   - In canal-irrigated Western UP (Ghaziabad / Meerut / Bulandshahr / Hapur): Typical cash rent is **₹20,000 to ₹35,000 per acre/year** for two-crop land (Sugarcane/Wheat/Paddy).
   - In semi-arid Central India (MP / Rajasthan): **₹8,000 to ₹18,000 per acre/year**.
   - *Advantage to Landowner:* Zero production risk, zero capital input, guaranteed cash flow.
2. **Sharecropping (Batai / Adhiya):**
   - Landowner and cultivator split the harvested crop 50:50 (or 1/3:2/3 depending on who pays for fertilizer, seed, and diesel).
   - *Risk:* Requires constant local physical monitoring at the threshing floor. High risk of underreporting of yields by tenant if the owner is non-resident.

## 5. Mandatory 10-Point Legal Due Diligence Checklist

Before paying any token money or signing an agreement to sell (*Bayanama / Satakhat*):
1. **Khatauni / Record of Rights (RoR):** Obtain certified digital extract from official state portal (e.g. `upbhulekh.gov.in` for UP). Verify that seller's name is recorded in Section 1 (Bhumidhar with transferable rights).
2. **Form 41 & Form 45 (Consolidation Records):** In states like UP, check CH Form 41/45 to trace plot consolidation history and verify original boundaries.
3. **Khasra / Shajra (Village Cadastral Map):** Ensure the plot has an identified physical shape and confirmed passage road (*Rasta* / *Chak-Marg*) linking to a PWD or village road.
4. **Litigation Search (Revenue Court Computerized Management System - RCCMS):** Check case records at Naib-Tehsildar, Tehsildar, SDM, and Collector courts to ensure no partition disputes (*Batawara* u/s 116) or eviction proceedings are active.
5. **Section 98 / 157A Clearance (Caste Restriction):** In UP, a Scheduled Caste (SC) Bhumidhar cannot transfer land to a non-SC without prior written sanction from the District Collector. Failure to obtain permission renders the registry void and land vests in the State Govt.
6. **Encumbrance Certificate (Sub-Registrar Office):** Minimum 30-year search to ensure no registered mortgage to a bank or prior agreement to sell exists.
7. **Kisan Credit Card (KCC) Bank Hypothecation:** Check whether the land has a bank charge (*Bandhak* / lien) entered in red ink in the remarks column (*Kafiyat*) of the Khatauni.
8. **Physical On-Ground Possession & Boundary Demarcation:** Commission an official boundary demarcation (*Paimaish / Medh-bandi*) via the Revenue Revenue Inspector (Kanungo/Lekhpal) before registry.
9. **No Ceiling Violations:** Verify the seller does not hold land in excess of statutory limits.
10. **Circle Rate & Stamp Duty Calculation:** Calculate applicable stamp duty (UP: typically 7% for men, 6% for women) + 1% registration fee based on actual circle rate.
