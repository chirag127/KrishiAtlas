# Owner Case Study: Strategic Decision Analysis Between Corporate Employment at TCS and Agricultural Enterprise in Ghaziabad

This case study is published with the explicit informed authorization of the project sponsor. It examines the real-world economic, legal, and operational dilemmas facing an early-career IT professional evaluating whether to remain in corporate technology employment or deploy capital and personal labour into family agricultural land in Western Uttar Pradesh. In strict accordance with research ethics, this document distinguishes between owner-reported statements, independently verified statutory and market benchmarks, deterministic financial models, and unanswered field questions. No personal identifiers, unverified cadastral coordinates, or fabricated family data are included.

## Baseline Context: Owner-Supplied Parameters vs. Field Realities

The owner reports full-time corporate technology employment at Tata Consultancy Services deployed at Bhubaneswar, Odisha, with a stated annual compensation package of approximately three lakh sixty thousand rupees Cost to Company. In an entry-level IT services structure, mandatory employer and employee Provident Fund contributions, professional taxes, and gratuity accruals yield an actual net take-home salary of approximately twenty-four thousand to twenty-six thousand rupees per month, translating to an annual liquid cash inflow of two lakh ninety thousand to three lakh ten thousand rupees.

After modest living expenses in Bhubaneswar, where shared housing, food, utilities, and transport consume between twelve thousand and sixteen thousand rupees monthly, the owner generates an annual liquid investable cash surplus of one lakh to one lakh forty thousand rupees. Crucially, corporate IT employment builds unpriced career equity through annual appraisal cycles, promotion pathways to Systems Engineer and Technical Lead within three to five years, and institutional creditworthiness.

In contrast, the owner's agricultural real estate baseline rests on unverified spoken parameters. The owner's hometown is near Ghaziabad, Western Uttar Pradesh, and the owner reports owning zero agricultural land personally. The owner's father reportedly possesses non-contiguous parcels in the broader Ghaziabad area, spoken of as roughly nine to ten local bighas and approximately five acres in total.

In Western Uttar Pradesh, land area conventions vary drastically by revenue custom. One standard Pukka Bigha equals 3,025 square yards or zero point six two five acres, meaning five acres equals exactly eight Pukka Bighas. Conversely, one local Kachha Bigha is traditionally one-third of a Pukka Bigha, meaning nine to ten Kachha Bighas equals only one point eight seven to two point zero eight acres. A holding spoken of as nine to ten bighas cannot simultaneously equal five acres under uniform revenue measurement. Until certified Khatauni extracts from the official state portal are inspected, the actual parcel acreage remains indeterminate.

Furthermore, under the Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code of 2006, succession to a male Bhumidhar occurs only upon his death. The owner possesses no independent legal standing to mortgage the land, execute binding leases, or construct permanent infrastructure without the father's written consent or registered power of attorney. If the land represents an undivided ancestral joint holding with uncles or cousins, developing any specific plot without a formal decree of partition under Section 116 risks immediate family litigation.

## Five Strategic Pathways Compared in Narrative Flow

A comprehensive financial comparison over a five-year horizon evaluates five strategic pathways available to the owner.

Pathway One represents the corporate status quo, where the owner remains exclusively employed at Tata Consultancy Services. The owner receives an initial take-home pay of three lakh rupees, accumulating twelve lakh five thousand rupees in liquid savings over five years alongside two lakh seventy thousand rupees in provident fund balances, achieving a total liquid net worth of fourteen lakh seventy-five thousand rupees with zero capital at risk.

Pathway Two models passive asset optimization, combining corporate employment with a formal cash lease of the family land in Ghaziabad. At prevailing two-crop irrigated lease rates of forty-five thousand rupees per acre, a five-acre holding generates two lakh rupees in net passive annual rental income. This lifts annual household cash inflow to five lakh rupees and expands five-year cumulative savings beyond twenty lakh fifty thousand rupees with zero capital at risk and zero tax liability under Section 10(1).

Pathway Three evaluates direct full-time farming of the five acres under sugarcane and wheat following resignation from employment. A five-acre holding produces a gross cash operating surplus of three lakh fifteen thousand rupees. However, subtracting the two lakh rupee opportunity cost of the land and thirty thousand rupees in machinery depreciation leaves a net economic reward for full-time personal labour of only eighty-five thousand rupees per year. Resigning from employment destroys three lakh rupees in net salary to earn eighty-five thousand rupees, resulting in a net economic loss of two lakh fifteen thousand rupees per year.

Pathway Four analyzes high-value peri-urban polyhouses or protected vegetable farming. While offering potential upside, protected horticulture requires capital investments of thirty to forty lakh rupees per acre and carries extreme operational risks: cladding tears, pest epidemics, and volatile mandi price crashes frequently induce heavy losses for novice cultivators lacking dedicated cold chains.

Pathway Five represents the hybrid value-chain strategy. The owner retains corporate employment, accumulates capital through Pathway Two, completes free government agricultural certifications, and in Year Three launches a reversible, high-margin one-acre vegetable seedling nursery in plug trays. Sourcing disease-free seedlings to local NCR growers generates two lakh thirty thousand rupees in net profit from a single acre, lifting total annual household income to six lakh ninety thousand rupees while preserving technical career progression.

## Recommended Action Plan and Execution Gates

The optimal risk-adjusted path follows a disciplined three-phase progression. 

During Phase One across Years One and Two, the owner retains full-time corporate employment in Bhubaneswar. Using state digital revenue portals and family visits, the owner obtains certified Khatauni records, village cadastral maps, and clarifies co-ownership rights. Simultaneously, the family formalizes a written cash lease agreement with an established local cultivator at prevailing rates of forty thousand to fifty thousand rupees per acre, securing reliable tax-exempt passive income. In addition, the owner completes free digital agricultural certifications via SWAYAM, agMOOCs, and weekend workshops at the district Krishi Vigyan Kendra in Muradnagar.

During Phase Two across Years Three and Four, with accumulated liquid savings, the owner reclaims exactly one acre of the most accessible irrigated parcel for a high-margin nursery pilot under trusted local supervision. Market off-take is validated in regional wholesale mandis without risking corporate livelihood.

During Phase Three in Year Five and beyond, the owner considers resigning from corporate employment only if the agricultural enterprise demonstrates audited net annual profits exceeding one point five times corporate salary for two consecutive seasons and a twelve-month liquid reserve is established.

## Mandatory Due Diligence Checklist

Before any capital commitment is made in Ghaziabad, five critical field parameters must be verified in narrative sequence. First, obtain the official Khasra and Gata numbers from tehsil revenue records and verify the exact surveyed area in hectares from the certified Khatauni extract. Second, verify the status of partition by metes and bounds to ensure ancestral co-sharers cannot disrupt operations. Third, collect groundwater samples from the active tubewell to test electrical conductivity, total dissolved solids, and sodium absorption ratios at the district Krishi Vigyan Kendra in Muradnagar. Fourth, inspect the village cadastral map to confirm an official chak road connects every parcel to a public road. Fifth, establish written family consensus regarding lease terms and enterprise management prior to deploying personal capital.
