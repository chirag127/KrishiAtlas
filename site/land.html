Agricultural Land Affordability & Cadastral Due Diligence

State statutory purchase restrictions, circle rates versus actual transaction values, and the hidden capital expenditure of developing remote rural land.

Can a Non-Farmer Buy Agricultural Land? (State-wise Rules)

Under Entry 18 of the State List (Seventh Schedule of the Constitution), agricultural land rights and tenure laws are strictly enacted at the State level.

State Permitted for Non-Farmer? Governing Statute & Ceilings Legal Caveats
Uttar Pradesh YES UP Revenue Code, 2006 (Section 89) Any Indian citizen can purchase up to 12.5 acres without prior administrative permission. SC land cannot be purchased by non-SC without Collector sanction (Sec 98).
Himachal Pradesh NO HP Tenancy and Land Reforms Act, 1972 (Section 118) Strict bar on non-agriculturists and non-residents. Outsiders can only buy tiny municipal residential plots or apply for commercial lease with State Cabinet sanction.
Uttarakhand RESTRICTED UPZA&LR Act (Adapted for Uttarakhand) Outsiders may buy only up to 250 sq. metres of agricultural land for residential use without DM permission. Larger purchases require District Magistrate sanction.
Maharashtra CONDITIONAL Maharashtra Tenancy & Agri Lands Act (Section 63) Historically restricted. Recent amendments allow non-farmers with annual non-agri income up to ₹7.5 lakh to purchase land for farming, or for bonafide industrial use (Sec 63-1A).
Karnataka YES (Reformed) Karnataka Land Reforms (Amendment) Act, 2020 Sections 79A, 79B, and 79C (which previously barred non-farmers and capped non-agri income) were repealed. Non-agriculturists can now purchase land up to ceiling limits.
Rajasthan YES Rajasthan Tenancy Act, 1955 No agriculturist-only restriction on non-tribal agricultural land; standard ceiling limits apply.

The Fallacy of "Cheap Land" — Hidden Development Capex

Promotional real estate brochures often market rural land in central India or arid zones at ₹3 to ₹5 lakh per acre. In agriculture, acquisition cost is rarely the primary barrier; developability capex and operational feasibility determine real viability:

1. Right of Way (Rasta / Road Access)

Cheap plots are frequently landlocked without an official Chak Road (*Chak-Marg*) on the village Shajra map. Purchasing an access easement from neighbours can cost 2x–4x the baseline land price, or leave the land inaccessible to tractors and harvesters.

2. Groundwater & Borewell Capex

In hard-rock basalt or granite aquifers, drilling 800–1,000 feet costs ₹2.0 to ₹3.5 lakh per borewell with zero geological guarantee. Without water, intensive two-crop agriculture is impossible.

3. Boundary Fencing & Wild Animals

Blue bull (*Nilgai*), wild boar, and stray cattle inflict total crop failure on unfenced plots. 7-strand barbed wire with RCC poles costs ₹1.5 to ₹2.5 lakh per acre.

4. Absentee Supervision & Pilferage

Remote farm owners face persistent pilferage of submersible pumps, copper cables, and harvests. Retaining a local caretaker costs ₹1.2 to ₹1.8 LPA, entirely eroding operational margins on holdings under 10 acres.
