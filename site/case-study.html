Owner Case Study: Strategic Decision Analysis Between Corporate Employment at TCS and Agricultural Enterprise in Ghaziabad

Official Notice on Public Authorization and Data Transparency:
This case study is published with the explicit informed authorization of the project sponsor. It examines the real-world economic, legal, and operational dilemmas facing an early-career IT professional evaluating whether to remain in corporate technology employment or deploy capital and personal labour into family agricultural land in Western Uttar Pradesh. In strict accordance with research ethics, this document distinguishes between owner-reported statements, independently verified statutory and market benchmarks, deterministic financial models, and unanswered field questions.

1. Baseline Context: Owner-Supplied Parameters vs. Field Realities

A. Corporate Employment Baseline (Owner-Supplied)

The owner currently works at Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) deployed at Bhubaneswar, Odisha, with a stated compensation package of approximately ₹3.60 Lakh per annum (CTC). In an entry-level IT services compensation structure, mandatory employer and employee Provident Fund contributions, professional taxes, and gratuity accruals yield an actual net take-home salary of approximately ₹24,000 to ₹26,000 per month (₹2.90 to ₹3.10 Lakh per year).

After modest living expenses in Bhubaneswar (shared housing, food, utilities, and transport consuming ₹12,000 to ₹16,000 monthly), the owner generates an annual liquid investable cash surplus of ₹1,00,000 to ₹1,40,000. Crucially, corporate IT employment builds unpriced career equity: annual appraisal cycles, promotion pathways to Systems Engineer and Technical Lead within three to five years, and institutional creditworthiness.

B. Agricultural Real Estate Baseline (Unverified Parameters)

The owner's hometown is near Ghaziabad, Western Uttar Pradesh. The owner reports owning zero agricultural land personally. The owner's father reportedly possesses non-contiguous parcels in the broader Ghaziabad area, spoken of as roughly nine to ten local bighas and approximately five acres in total.

Critical Measurement Ambiguity:
In Western Uttar Pradesh, land area conventions vary drastically by revenue custom. One standard Pukka Bigha equals 3,025 square yards (0.625 acres), meaning five acres equals exactly eight Pukka Bighas. Conversely, one local Kachha Bigha is traditionally one-third of a Pukka Bigha (approx. 0.208 acres), meaning nine to ten Kachha Bighas equals only 1.87 to 2.08 acres. A holding spoken of as "nine to ten bighas" cannot equal "five acres" under uniform revenue measurement. Until certified Khatauni extracts from upbhulekh.gov.in are obtained, the actual parcel acreage remains indeterminate.

3. Recommended Action Plan: The Hybrid Value-Chain Pathway

The optimal risk-adjusted path is not an emotional resignation from TCS, but a disciplined three-phase progression:

  1. Phase 1 (Years 1–2) — Retain, Secure, and Verify: Retain full-time TCS employment in Bhubaneswar. Use online revenue tools (`upbhulekh.gov.in`) and family visits to obtain certified digital Khatauni records, village Shajra cadastral maps, and clarify co-ownership rights. Formalize a written cash lease agreement (*Theka*) with an established local cultivator for the family land at prevailing Western UP rates (₹28,000 to ₹35,000 per acre per year), generating ₹1.30 to ₹1.50 LPA in pure tax-exempt passive income.
  2. Phase 2 (Years 3–4) — The Reversible 1-Acre Pilot: Complete free agricultural courses via SWAYAM and agMOOCs (IIT Kanpur). With ₹5 Lakh in accumulated liquid savings, reclaim exactly 1 acre of the most accessible irrigated parcel for a high-margin pilot (e.g. vegetable seedling nursery in plug trays or specialty herbs) under local family supervision. Test real off-take at Sahibabad and Ghazipur mandis without risking corporate livelihood.
  3. Phase 3 (Year 5+) — Decision Gate: Only consider leaving corporate employment if validated annual net farm profits exceed 1.5 times corporate take-home pay for two consecutive seasons and a 12-month liquid reserve is in place.

4. Unanswered Due Diligence Checklist

Before any investment or operational shift occurs in Ghaziabad, verify:

  • Official Khasra and Gata numbers from Modinagar / Sadar / Loni tehsil records.
  • Exact surveyed area in Hectares from certified Khatauni extract.
  • Status of partition by metes and bounds (*Batawara* u/s 116 of UP Revenue Code, 2006).
  • Borewell water quality test (pH, EC, heavy metal TDS) conducted at KVK Muradnagar.
  • Confirmed approach road (*Chak road*) connecting every parcel to a public PWD road.
